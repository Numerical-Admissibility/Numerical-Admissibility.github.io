AP-1

An open standard for evaluating numerical admissibility and operand provenance in AI systems operating in mission-critical environments.

v1.2 is the version in force. v1.3 is published as a draft for public comment and is not adopted.
Comment window closes 30 September 2026.

The Governance Challenge

In regulated operational environments, an agent may originate an operand itself and pass it to a correctly functioning deterministic tool. The tool-execution record remains valid, because a computation genuinely occurred. What it fails to establish is that the operand entering the computation was authoritative.

Instructing a model not to originate operands is merely a policy; establishing after execution that every consequential operand can be traced to an authoritative source is a control.

A correct numerical output does not, by itself, establish that the deployed system executed the deterministic computation required to produce it. That property is not a property of the output, and cannot be established by inspecting the answer.

Evaluation Dimensions

AP-1 defines seven dimensions. A conformant evaluation reports each separately, with sample size and evidence class. The standard produces no aggregate score and sets no pass mark. D7.2(a) below is a sub-measure of D7 proposed in the v1.3 draft.

D1  Accuracy

Whether the figure is correct against ground truth reproducible from the published fixture.

D2  Determinism

Whether an identical question returns an identical answer across repeated runs, and by what mechanism — structurally, by configuration, or merely observed.

D3  Provenance

Whether the system can produce the source and the operation for every figure it states.

D4  Refusal Integrity

Whether the system declines when the data cannot support an answer, rather than producing one.

D5  Adversarial Resistance

Whether the system holds under sustained pressure to state a figure that does not exist.

D6  Conflicting Input

Whether contradictory, stale, partial or out-of-range source data is detected rather than computed over silently.

D7  Computation Invocation

Whether the required deterministic computation actually executed — and whether invocation is guaranteed rather than merely observed.

D7.2(a)  Operand Provenance

Whether every numerical operand entering a deterministic computation traces to an authoritative source or a declared transformation.

Sub-measure of D7 — proposed in v1.3

What AP-1 Is Not

AP-1 issues no certification, maintains no registry, and charges no fee. It produces a per-dimension profile, not a score, and sets no threshold — the reader of the profile determines whether the evidence is adequate for the use in question.

It defines no pass criterion for any dimension. There is no verdict to issue and no mark to award; a system is described, not graded. It is architecture-neutral and domain-neutral, and any system may be evaluated under it, including its author's. The standard states its own defeat condition and publishes it, so that any party may attempt refutation.

AP-1 addresses numerical admissibility. It does not address fairness, bias, model drift, consumer explainability, third-party risk or model lifecycle management.

Origin and Disclosure

AP-1 was authored by ZORRZ Financial Inc., which develops systems intended to satisfy the standard. The standard and its reference implementation are published and maintained by their author. No independent steward currently holds them.

That relationship is disclosed here and in the standard's own governance section, because a measurement standard whose author will not be measured by it is a brochure. AP-1 is designed to be applied to any system, including its author's, and is free permanently under CC-BY 4.0.

No independent application of the standard has yet occurred. The reference evaluation is a single author-run case study whose defects are published in full as an erratum, and whose affected results are withdrawn.

The reference implementation is under private technical review ahead of public release. Its fixtures and expected values were authored by an AI coding agent rather than derived from an independent source; no language model executes during an evaluation run.
